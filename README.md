## A 2003 classic, reborn for Windows 11

Back in 2003, Tiger Technologies' **Holiday Lights** put strings of blinking bulbs around Windows desktops. It
had 49 built-in bulbs, a library of more than 1,500 bulbs drawn by its fans, a little Music Box of holiday MIDI songs
and a snowy screen saver. Then Windows moved on, and the old lights ended up blurry, squeezed into a corner of one
screen, or not working at all.

**Holiday Lights 6** is a from-scratch rebuild for today's Windows 11 PCs, with the same bulbs, the same five flash
patterns down to the millisecond, the same Music Box songs and the same screen saver, and a lot more:

* **Sharp on 4K and high-DPI screens.** The pixel art is upscaled with MMPX, so the bulbs keep their hand-drawn look
  at 125 %, 150 % or 200 % scaling.
* **Every monitor gets lights,** whatever its size, scaling or position. You can also wrap all of your monitors in
  one big wreath.
* **The bulbs really glow.** Lit bulbs throw soft light onto your wallpaper, and they fade on and off like real bulbs.
* **It sits behind your desktop icons,** like 2003, but it never takes a click, never steals focus and gets out of
  the way of full-screen games, videos and presentations.

## Why you'll love it

### It glows, it twinkles, it chases

Eleven flash patterns: the five classics from 2003 (**Don't Flash**, **Flash Together**, **Alternating**, **Bulb
Chase** and **Random Flashing**, timed exactly like the original) and six new ones (**Twinkle**, **Slow Glow**,
**Chase Around the Screen**, **Dance to the Music**, **Waves** and **Combination**). Pick a speed, and pick how much glow
you like.

### Themes that follow the calendar

Nineteen themes are included: the eleven classics from 2003 (Christmas 1, Christmas 2, Halloween, Thanksgiving,
Valentine's Day, Easter Eggs, St. Patrick's Day, July 4th, New Year, Chanukah and Blank Slate) and eight new ones such

## Everything it does

**Lights**

* 1,550 bulbs: the 49 built-in bulbs and 1,501 add-on bulbs from Holiday Lights 5.4, plus any `.bul` files or GIF
  animations you add.
* Four edges with up to six bulbs each and four corner bulbs; one arrangement for all of your displays.
* 11 flash patterns, 9 speeds, smooth fading, and glow (Off, Soft or Bright). Looks: **Modern Glow** (default),
  **Bright Glow**, **Classic 2003** and Custom. Four bulb sizes.
* Drawn behind your desktop icons (default), in front of them, or on top of all windows (always below the taskbar).
  The lights never take clicks or focus. **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+B** switches between the desktop and on top.
* Every display, each at its own scaling, or **All Displays Together** as one wreath; turn lights off on any display.

**Themes**

* 19 themes, your own saved themes, and Recent Settings that keeps your last five setups.
* Automatic themes on holidays (New Year, Valentine's Day, St. Patrick's Day, Easter, July 4th, Halloween,
  Thanksgiving, Christmas, plus Chanukah if you check it), with **Classic Lights** between holidays and dates that
  follow your region.

**Music Box**

* 46 bundled holiday songs, and your own songs in MIDI, MP3, WMA, WAV, M4A, AIFF and AU formats.
* Play always, intermittently, only while the screen saver is on (or off), or never; volume, Previous, Pause and
  Next Song.
* **Dance to the Music:** the lights follow the notes of the song.

**Screen saver**

* Your bulbs around 24 animations (Snow, Snow Flakes, Balloons, Leaves, Santa, Halloween, Valentine's Hearts and more,
  or any add-on bulb), four motion styles, 11 pictures or your own, and your message in any font and color.
* Shows on every monitor, with a live preview in Settings.

**Bulb Factory and Bulb Editing**

* Search, categories, favorites, try-on, Undo and Redo, and drag and drop or keyboard-only editing.
* Turn GIF animations into bulbs, edit flavors and credits, and export `.bul` files that work in 5.4 too.

**Respectful by design**

* **Private and offline:** no internet connection, no telemetry, no update checks, no account. Logs stay on your PC.
* **Light on your PC:** the lights are drawn with DirectComposition on the GPU, and the CPU only wakes up at the flash
  steps. The design budget is under 0.5 % of one CPU core with the default theme on two 4K displays, and nothing at
  all while the lights are hidden.
* **Out of the way:** the lights step aside on a display while a full-screen app or game runs there, during
  presentations, and while your PC is locked. When Windows Energy Saver is on they use less power.
* **Accessible:** every feature works with the keyboard and with Narrator or NVDA, High Contrast is supported, and
  **Limit Flashing to 3 Flashes per Second** keeps the lights festive for photosensitive viewers.
* **Your 2003 setup comes along:** if Holiday Lights 5.4 is on your PC, your settings, themes, categories and your own
  bulbs, songs and pictures are imported on first start.

## Download and install

1. Go to the **[Releases page](https://github.com/starrlord/holidaylights/releases)** and download
   **`HolidayLights-<version>-win-x64.zip`** from the latest release.
2. Extract the zip (right-click it, then **Extract All**).
3. In the extracted folder, double-click **`Setup.exe`**.

Holiday Lights installs for you only, in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\HolidayLights`, so **no administrator rights** are
needed. The usual download includes everything Holiday Lights needs, so it works on any 64-bit Windows 11 PC, even
offline. If your PC already has the [.NET 10 Desktop Runtime (x64)](https://dotnet.microsoft.com/download/dotnet/10.0),
you can take the smaller `HolidayLights-<version>-win-x64-framework-dependent.zip` instead. Each release lists SHA-256
checksums for both zips.

> [!NOTE]
> Holiday Lights is not code-signed, so the first time you run `Setup.exe`, Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows
> protected your PC". Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**System requirements:** 64-bit Windows 11. Holiday Lights works with any number of displays, at any scaling.

## Quick start

1. **The lights are on.** After setup, Holiday Lights starts and powers up its lights around every display, and the
   Welcome card shows where to find it. With automatic themes on, you get the season's theme right away.
2. **Click the red bulb** in the notification area (next to the clock; if you don't see it, select **^**) to open the
   menu. Choose **Themes** to change the theme, or **Show Lights** to turn the lights off and on.
3. **Double-click the red bulb** (or choose **Holiday Lights Settings...**) to open Settings. On the **Bulb Factory**
   page, double-click any bulb to put it around the whole frame.
4. **Want music?** Turn on **Play Holiday Music** on the Welcome card, in the menu or on the **Music Box** page.
5. **Want the screen saver?** On the **Screen Saver** page, choose **Use Holiday Lights as My Screen Saver**.

Holiday Lights starts with Windows (you can turn that off on the Welcome card or the General page), and Help is built
in: choose **Holiday Lights Help** in the menu, or press **F1** in Settings.

## FAQ

<details>
<summary><b>Will it slow down my PC?</b></summary>

No. The lights are drawn by the GPU through DirectComposition, and the CPU only works when the bulbs change: the design
budget is under 0.5 % of one CPU core with the default theme on two 4K displays. When the lights are hidden (a
full-screen game, a locked PC, a display that is off), nothing is drawn at all. On a laptop with Energy Saver on,
Holiday Lights automatically uses less power; you can also choose to stop the flashing or turn the lights off.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Does it work with several monitors?</b></summary>

Yes. Every display gets its own complete frame at its own scaling, wherever it is (left of, right of or above the main
display, 4K or not). On the General page you can turn the lights off for any display, choose **Main Display Only**
like 2003, or choose **All Displays Together** to put one wreath around all of your monitors.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Will the lights cover my windows?</b></summary>

Not unless you want them to. By default the bulbs are drawn on the desktop behind your icons, so every window covers
them. You can put them in front of the icons, or on top of all windows (they always stay below the taskbar and never
take a click). **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+B** switches between the desktop and on top. In every mode, the lights hide on a
display while a full-screen app or game runs there, and during presentations.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Will it play music when I don't expect it?</b></summary>

No. Music is off until you turn it on, and Holiday Lights never changes your Windows settings unless you ask.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>I used Holiday Lights 5.4. Do I lose my setup?</b></summary>

No. On first start, Holiday Lights 6 imports your 5.4 settings, themes, categories and the bulbs, songs and pictures
you added. If you never changed 5.4's settings, you get the new seasonal look, and **Use My 2003 Lights** on the
Welcome card brings back the exact 2003 setup. The **Classic 2003** look restores the original crisp, glow-free bulbs.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Does it collect any data?</b></summary>

No. Holiday Lights never connects to the internet: no telemetry, no update checks, no account. The only web link in
the app is the project page in the About window, and it opens only when you click it.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How do I uninstall it?</b></summary>

Open **Settings > Apps > Installed apps** in Windows, find **Holiday Lights** and choose **Uninstall**. The uninstaller
removes the program, its screen saver, its startup entry and its `.bul` file association, and puts back the screen
saver you had before. It asks before it removes your own bulbs, songs and pictures (kept in
`Documents\Holiday Lights`), and it removes your settings and themes only if you check that box.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Is it really free?</b></summary>

Yes. No trial, no registration, no ads. The source code is MIT-licensed, and the classic content is included with
permission (see [License](#license)).
</details>

## Build from source

You need Windows 11 and the [.NET 10 SDK](https://dotnet.microsoft.com/download/dotnet/10.0) (10.0.400 or later, see
`global.json`).

```powershell
git clone https://github.com/starrlord/holidaylights.git
cd holidaylights
dotnet build HolidayLights.sln -c Release
dotnet test HolidayLights.sln -c Release --filter "Category!=Live"
```

To try a build without touching your real settings, startup entry, screen saver or file associations, run it with a
private data folder:

```powershell
.\src\HolidayLights.App\bin\Release\net10.0-windows10.0.26100.0\HolidayLights.exe --data-root "$env:TEMP\hl-try" --no-system-changes
```

`pwsh tools/publish/publish.ps1` builds the distributable folder and zip (`HolidayLights.exe`, `Setup.exe` and the
bundled content). Releases are built by GitHub Actions when a `v*` tag is pushed; see
[docs/RELEASING.md](docs/RELEASING.md).

| Folder | What's inside |
|---|---|
| `src/HolidayLights.Core` | Bulb files and the bulb library, layout, flash patterns, sprites and glow, themes and the holiday calendar, settings |
| `src/HolidayLights.Rendering` | The DirectComposition presenter: the lights behind the icons, power-up and theme transitions |
| `src/HolidayLights.Platform` | Displays, the desktop host, hot keys, pausing rules, installation helpers |
| `src/HolidayLights.Audio` | The Music Box: MIDI and audio playback, Dance to the Music |
| `src/HolidayLights.App` | The WPF app: tray menu, Settings, Bulb Editing, screen saver, Help, installer |
| `tests/HolidayLights.Tests` | xUnit tests, including golden data that pins the 2003 layouts and flash patterns |
| `content` | The bundled bulbs, songs and pictures ([content/README.md](content/README.md)) |
| `docs` | [Product spec](docs/PRODUCT-SPEC.md), [architecture](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md), [contracts](docs/CONTRACTS.md), [user guide](docs/USER-GUIDE.md) |
